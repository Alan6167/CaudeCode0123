# Lite EEAT Style Guide

This guide supports `SKILL.md` with tone comparisons, before/after examples, and a quick reference for keeping content trust-signal-rich without sliding into audit-report tone.

## Why Lite EEAT?

EEAT stands for **Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness** — Google's quality framework for content.

A "heavy" EEAT approach (certificate numbers, audit report links, detailed test methodology references) is impressive but:

- Hard to source: most public research can only verify what's on the official website
- Hard to read: stacks of jargon alienate general buyers
- Hard to maintain: facts go stale and need re-verification

**Lite EEAT keeps the four signals but sources them from publicly available information and expresses them in approachable language.**

## The Four Signals — Lite Version

| Signal | Heavy version | Lite version |
|---|---|---|
| **Experience** | "We conducted on-site factory audits at 12 suppliers" | "Based on a review of each company's official website and general sourcing knowledge in the industry" |
| **Expertise** | Migration testing per LFGB §30/§31; 304-grade electro-polished stainless | "LFGB compliant (per company site)"; "Uses 304-grade stainless steel" |
| **Authoritativeness** | Certificate IDs, validity dates, third-party audit report links | Certification names + official website URL |
| **Trustworthiness** | Reviewer bio, methodology appendix, conflict-of-interest statement | Byline + update date + short methodology paragraph + source disclosure at the end |

## Tone Reference Table

Use the middle column. The left is over-engineered, the right is marketing fluff.

| Over-professional | Lite EEAT (target) | Over-marketing (avoid) |
|---|---|---|
| "ISO 9001:2015 certified by TÜV, valid through 2027" | "ISO 9001 certified" | "World-class quality" |
| "Migration testing per LFGB §30/31 passed 2024-11" | "LFGB compliant" | "Premium food-safe" |
| "MOQ: 3,000 pcs ± 10% tolerance" | "MOQ usually around 3,000 pieces" | "Flexible MOQ" |
| "Verified exports of 2.3M units/year per 2024 BSCI audit" | "A large-scale exporter focused on overseas brands" | "Global market leader" |
| "Double-wall vacuum-insulated, 24-hr thermal retention (per spec sheet)" | "Double-wall vacuum insulation, which keeps drinks hot or cold longer" | "Revolutionary temperature tech" |
| "Dedicated OEM/ODM pipeline with in-house tooling" | "Offers OEM (branded production) and ODM (custom design) services" | "Full-service solutions" |

## Jargon — Explain Once, Use Normally

On first appearance, give a short in-parentheses explanation. After that, use the term freely.

**Approved short glossary:**

- **OEM** (building products with your brand on them)
- **ODM** (they design the product, you brand and sell it)
- **MOQ** (the smallest order size a factory accepts)
- **Lead time** (how long from confirmed order to shipment)
- **Double-wall vacuum** (two walls of steel with vacuum between, keeps drinks hot or cold longer)
- **BPA-free** (made without Bisphenol A, a plastic additive avoided for food contact)
- **LFGB / FDA 21 CFR 177** (EU and US food-safety rules for materials that touch food or drink)

Do not re-explain a term after the first occurrence. Do not explain terms that will clearly be understood from context alone.

## Profile Voice — Before/After

### Before (heavy + promotional)

> **Everich & Tomic Housewares Co., Ltd.**
>
> **Certifications:** ISO 9001:2015, BSCI, Sedex, SMETA, FDA 21 CFR 177, LFGB §30/31
>
> A world-class, industry-leading manufacturer of cutting-edge drinkware solutions, Everich empowers global brands with a robust, end-to-end OEM/ODM ecosystem.

### After (Lite EEAT)

> ### 1. Everich
>
> **Website:** everich.com · **Based in:** Hangzhou, China · **Since:** 2000
>
> One of the larger drinkware exporters in China, Everich works mainly with overseas brands on custom stainless steel tumblers and water bottles.
>
> **What makes them stand out**
>
> Their product catalog is broad — covering sports bottles, insulated tumblers, kids' bottles, and food containers. They list multiple food-safety certifications (FDA, LFGB) and ethical audits (BSCI, Sedex) on their site, which matters if you're selling into the US or EU.
>
> **Good fit if you're...**
>
> - A mid-to-large brand running custom OEM programs
> - Ordering in the 3,000+ piece range per SKU
> - Looking for a supplier with English-fluent sales support
>
> **Worth knowing**
>
> Their website doesn't always show current lead times — expect to confirm production schedules directly during sampling, especially for peak season.

Notice: the Lite version uses the same facts, but the voice is advisor-like, the facts are stated simply, and the "Worth knowing" line is what separates it from brochure copy.

## FAQ Voice — Before/After

### Before (dictionary-flat)

> **What is MOQ?**
> MOQ is the minimum order quantity a manufacturer accepts for production.

### After (Lite EEAT)

> **What's a realistic minimum order when sourcing tumblers from China?**
> For most China-based factories, expect minimums around 1,000–3,000 pieces if you want a custom logo. Stock designs without branding can sometimes go lower. If a supplier says "no minimum" on a custom OEM run, they're usually a trading company reselling from someone else — not the factory itself.

**The two ingredients:**

1. A direct answer in plain language
2. One extra sentence that shows experience (a nuance, a buyer's gotcha, a common misconception)

## Quick Self-Check

Before finalizing, scan the draft for these red flags:

- [ ] Any certificate number or validity date you didn't actually verify? → Remove
- [ ] Any "No.1", "leading", "world-class" claims without attribution? → Remove or attribute
- [ ] Any company profile that only praises and has no "Worth knowing" note? → Add one honest note
- [ ] First use of MOQ/OEM/ODM without a short explanation? → Add parenthetical once
- [ ] Missing byline, missing update date, missing source disclosure? → Add them

## Golden Rule

**If a sentence could appear in the company's own marketing brochure, rewrite it.**
Facts stay; promotional energy goes.
