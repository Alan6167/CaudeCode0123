    What's hiding in your photos?

    Every photo from a phone or camera carries invisible metadata. When you share the file, all of it goes along:

    • GPS location — exact coordinates of where the photo was taken, often precise enough to reveal your home address.
    • Date and time — when the photo was taken, down to the second, revealing your routines and whereabouts.
    • Device details — your phone or camera make and model, and sometimes the lens and serial number.
    • Software traces — which apps edited the photo, and IPTC/XMP records added by editing tools.
    • Hidden extras — some phones append extra data after the image itself, such as embedded video clips from motion photos. This tool strips those too.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is photo metadata (EXIF data)?

    EXIF metadata is hidden information that cameras and phones embed in every photo: GPS coordinates of where it was taken, the exact date and time, your camera or phone model, lens details, and the software used to edit it. It travels with the file when you share it — unless you remove it first.

    Can people really see where a photo was taken?

    Yes. If a photo still contains GPS metadata, anyone who receives the file can read the exact coordinates with free tools — often precise enough to identify your home address. Most social networks strip metadata on upload, but files shared by email, cloud drive, or messaging apps usually keep it.

    Does removing metadata reduce image quality?

    No. This tool never decodes or re-encodes your image. It parses the file structure and removes only the metadata sections, so the picture data stays bit-for-bit identical to the original. Files usually get a little smaller, never worse.

    Are my photos uploaded to a server?

    No. Everything runs in JavaScript inside your browser. Your photos never leave your device — you can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads and the tool keeps working. That's the whole point: a privacy tool shouldn't ask you to hand over your files.

    Which file formats are supported?

    JPG/JPEG, PNG, and WebP — the formats used by virtually every website and messaging app. HEIC (the iPhone camera default) is not supported yet: set Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible, or share the photo as JPEG first.

    What exactly gets removed?

    EXIF data (including GPS location, camera and lens details, timestamps), XMP and IPTC/Photoshop data, embedded comments, and hidden data appended after the image ends — such as the video clip inside some motion photos. Color profiles (ICC) are kept so your photo looks exactly the same everywhere.

    How do I remove metadata from photos on iPhone or Android?

    This page works in your phone's browser — open it, tap the drop area, and pick photos from your gallery. iOS can natively remove location only (share sheet → Options → toggle Location off). On Android, Google Photos can strip location before sharing, but removing the full EXIF record usually requires a tool like this one.

    Why is there an option to keep the orientation tag?

    Phones often save photos rotated and rely on a tiny orientation tag to display them upright. Strip it, and some apps show the photo sideways. The tag is a single number with zero personal information, so keeping it is recommended — but purists can untick the option for a fully metadata-free file.
